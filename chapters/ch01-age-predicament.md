#### 1.1 The Phenomenon: Spiritual Disorientation, Erosion of Trust, and the Impact of AI

1.1.1 Inner Emptiness Amid Material Abundance

Our external world is unprecedentedly prosperous: technology brings convenience, and material goods are abundant. However, in sharp contrast, inner anxiety, emptiness, feelings of meaninglessness, and depressive emotions are spreading widely. We possess more, yet seem unable to access deeper happiness and fulfillment. This reveals a core paradox: external accumulation cannot fill internal voids.

1.1.2 A Crisis of Interpersonal Trust

In the era of social media, we have hundreds or thousands of “friends,” yet intimate confidants for deep, heartfelt connections have become few and far between. The information explosion has led to extreme polarization and conflicting viewpoints, making basic trust between people fragile. Commercial fraud and emotional betrayal have become commonplace, rendering trust an increasingly scarce form of social capital. We live amidst superficial connections yet experience profound loneliness and alienation.

1.1.3 Existential Anxiety Posed by Artificial Intelligence

The rapid development of Artificial Intelligence (AI) is no longer distant science fiction; it is actively permeating and reshaping every industry. Knowledge-based, pattern-driven white-collar work faces the risk of being replaced. This is not merely a professional challenge; it triggers a deeper identity crisis and existential anxiety: If the knowledge, logic, skills, and even creativity that humans pride themselves on can be surpassed by machines, then where does our unique value lie? Upon what foundation should our confidence be built?

#### 1.2 Misconceptions: Dying of Thirst in an Ocean of Information

Facing these, we instinctively seek answers externally. We continuously learn, continuously “recharge” ourselves. Yet, in the process of searching, we often fall into deeper misconceptions.

1.2.1 Information Overload and Scarcity of Wisdom

Today, the internet places us in an ocean of information; the volume of information we encounter daily is thousands of times greater than what was faced by ancient people. Yet, we find it harder to obtain true wisdom to guide our lives. We become repositories of facts, “knowing” many principles, but not truly wise individuals who can embody those principles in the quality of their lives. We collect countless articles and courses, yet our inner selves become more confused. This is like holding an encyclopedia yet remaining unable to quench one’s thirst.

1.2.2 Measuring Ultimate Truth with the Yardstick of Science

Modern science, based on the paradigm of “observability and repeatability,” has achieved glorious successes in exploring and transforming the material world. We respect this. However, this set of tools excels at studying “matter,” but for phenomena that transcend pure matter—such as “consciousness,” “energy,” and “the nature of life”—current methods are like “the blind men and the elephant.” Attempting to use the “scientific” yardstick—a tool only a few hundred years old designed to study matter—to examine or negate the ultimate wisdom about life’s truth (primarily concerning consciousness and energy) realized by sages over millennia is an arrogant category error. It is like asking an elementary school student who only knows how to measure volume with a graduated cylinder to evaluate a postdoctoral philosophical dissertation on “justice”—a futile endeavor.
